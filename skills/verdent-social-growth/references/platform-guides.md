# Verdent Platform Guides

Use the shared confirmed fact base, then rewrite for the platform. These are operating defaults, not claims about secret platform algorithms.

Before using these defaults, analyze the two latest first-party Verdent posts on the target platform. Let those samples control current cadence and formatting when they are verifiable and compatible with the task type. Never reuse their facts or claims.

## X / Twitter

### Formal release or model launch

- Name the version, feature, or model in the first line.
- Default to one post within 280 characters when the key value fits.
- Use two to four short paragraphs for a longer post only when context is necessary.
- Focus on one primary value and at most two supporting details.
- A question is optional. Do not force debate into release news.
- Use zero hashtags by default. Use at most two when they identify an active Verdent campaign.
- Use the website CTA when a reader can act immediately. Omit it when availability is pending.
- The July 2026 release baseline favors one compact, image-supported paragraph: literal availability, one primary workload, one useful detail or caveat, then a direct product action.
- When announcing two models together, give each a distinct role instead of blending their capabilities into one claim.

### Editorial or builder story

- Lead with a concrete insight, result, visual moment, or developer pain.
- Use a V-series label only when the task explicitly belongs to that series.
- Ground opinions in approved interviews, dogfooding, support themes, or first-hand shipping experience.
- Do not invent numbers to make a hook stronger.
- Invite a specific response when it fits the content.

## Discord

- Treat readers as existing community members.
- Open with the update in plain language. Use `Hi @everyone` only when the channel policy and importance justify a mass mention.
- Keep routine announcements around 80 to 180 words.
- Use bold labels and bullets when there are multiple actions or controls.
- Use zero to three functional emoji, not decorative emoji strings.
- State rollout, access limits, known issues, and required user action explicitly.
- End with the next action or a specific feedback request.

## LinkedIn

- Explain what changed, why it matters, and where it fits in a professional workflow.
- Use 120 to 220 words for a model update and 180 to 320 for a version, launch, or event story.
- Keep paragraphs to one to three sentences.
- Prefer concrete workflow language over slogans.
- Mention a model provider or partner account only when coordinated or clearly useful.
- Use zero to three relevant hashtags at the end. Do not place hashtags inside sentences.
- Link to Verdent when the post has a clear trial or product CTA.
- The July 2026 model baseline uses a literal opening, a capability-to-workflow explanation, and one direct CTA without a forced question or hashtag block.
- The July 2026 product-release baseline can be longer: identify the workflow friction, explain each change separately, and close on the combined outcome.

## Reddit

- Always output `Reddit Title:` and `Reddit Body:` separately.
- Use a literal, searchable title naming the model, version, or feature.
- State affiliation when community rules or context require it.
- Keep the body grounded, normally 80 to 200 words.
- Explain actual use cases and limitations. Avoid ad-like superlatives.
- Do not paste a marketing CTA after every paragraph. Include a link only when it adds practical value or the community rules allow it.
- Ask one specific feedback question when genuine product feedback is useful.
- Check the target subreddit's current rules before posting.
- The July 2026 baseline uses a transparent first-person product voice, short factual paragraphs, and concrete use cases. A feedback question belongs only when the team genuinely wants responses.

## Instagram

Choose the format before writing.

### Model or feature card

- Put the model/feature and availability state on the visual.
- Keep the caption concise: what changed, one to two use cases, and how to try it.
- Use five to eight relevant hashtags. Avoid generic hashtag walls.

### Reel or build story

- On-screen hook: up to ten words and understandable without audio.
- Caption arc: result or tension, what was built, one concrete detail, specific question.
- Write for the visual shown. Do not paste the LinkedIn post.
- Use six to ten relevant hashtags, including Verdent only when Verdent is visibly part of the build.
- Use `link in bio` only if the current profile setup supports the intended destination.

## TikTok

- Treat the video as primary and the caption as supporting context.
- Provide: on-screen hook, 20 to 45 second shot list, voiceover, caption, and three to six hashtags.
- Show the result in the first three seconds, then the problem and build process.
- Keep the hook to seven words when possible.
- Use short, spoken English and a natural specific CTA.
- Avoid generic intros, feature-list voiceover, and unsupported time-saving claims.
- Current account-specific guidance is provisional until first-party post exports and performance data are available.

## CTA distribution

- X: website link when the product is available; otherwise a focused reply question.
- Discord: direct product action or feedback request.
- LinkedIn: website or relevant announcement page.
- Reddit: optional and contextual, never mechanically repeated.
- Instagram: visual/caption CTA; use bio-link language only when verified.
- TikTok: comment prompt or profile action appropriate to the current profile configuration.

One campaign does not need the same CTA on every platform.
